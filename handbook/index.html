Ondine Partnerships Operating Handbook

Version 3.2, effective Mon 2 Nov 2026. Owner: Hanne Sorensen (Legal).

1. About this handbook

This is the Ondine Partnerships Operating Handbook, version 3.2, effective Mon 2 Nov 2026. Owner: Hanne Sorensen (Legal). It replaces version 3.1, which stays online for reference only.

It covers how we find, contract, brief, pay and offboard creators, and how we use their content. Brand, Paid Social, Finance and Legal all work from it.

If an email, chat, deck, sheet or older copy disagrees with this handbook, this handbook wins, unless Appendix B says otherwise. Read the appendices: several rules in section 7 point to them.

Questions about this handbook go to Legal. Suggested changes go into the handbook review document, which Legal goes through at the end of each quarter. Nothing in that review document is in effect until it is published here.

2. Who we work with

We work with creators on three kinds of deal: product gifting, paid posts, and paid usage of their content in our ads. A creator can have more than one kind of deal at once, and each kind has its own rules in this handbook.

Tier A creators have more than 250,000 followers on their main platform. Tier B have 50,000 to 250,000. Tier C have fewer than 50,000. Tiers decide the briefing template, the gifting allowance and who signs the brief, not rights or fees.

Every creator we pay has a page in the Creator Rights database. Their contract fields live there and nowhere else. A spreadsheet, chat or deck that lists a creator's rate or term is a copy, and copies go out of date.

We do not work with creators under 18, with creators who have posted a skincare claim we would not allow in the last six months, or with creators who refuse to disclose paid posts.

3. Onboarding a creator

Onboarding takes five steps: a fit check by Brand, a rate conversation, a contract from the template in section 4, a database page, and a welcome pack. Nobody gets paid or briefed before their database page exists.

The fit check looks at the last 30 posts. Brand records the result in the onboarding tracker, not in the database.

The welcome pack goes out within five business days of signature. It has the brand book, the claims guide in section 14 and the creator's first brief.

Creators added to the database mid-month are not part of that month's rights sweep unless they already have ads in the export.

4. Contract templates

There are three templates: gifting, paid post and paid usage. Paid usage contracts set the assets, channels, territory, term length in days, licence type (term or perpetual) and the monthly extension rate. Any of those fields can be changed later by a signed amendment.

Templates are kept in the Legal drive. Never edit a signed contract. Changes go into an amendment, which Legal drafts and both sides sign.

A perpetual licence is rare and needs Legal and the CFO to agree. It covers the listed assets forever on the listed channels and territory.

If a contract field is empty, it is empty on purpose until Legal fills it. Don't copy a value across from another creator or from an old rate card.

5. Negotiation

Opening offers for paid posts follow the tier guide: Tier A from $1,500, Tier B from $600, Tier C from $250 per post. Paid usage rates are negotiated separately and written into the contract.

Agents often ask for a higher rate in exchange for a longer term or more channels. That conversation is fine, but nothing changes until Legal turns it into a signed amendment.

Discounts offered by an agent in an email are an opening position, not an agreement. Log them in the negotiation tracker and pass them to Legal.

6. Briefs and content approval

Briefs go out at least ten business days before a post is due. Creators send drafts for approval, and Brand replies within two business days.

Approval is about brand fit and claims, not rights. An approved post can still have no paid usage rights.

If Brand asks for changes twice and the creator declines, the post is cancelled and the creator is paid 50% of the post fee.

7. Paid usage rights

This section is the rulebook for the monthly rights sweep. Follow it in order. Where it points to an appendix, the appendix is part of the rule.

7.1 What counts

Paid usage means an ad in the Meta Ads or TikTok Ads tab of the rights export that uses a creator's asset. TikTok rows show the asset ID. Meta rows show a creative ID, and the Creative Library tab says which asset each creative uses. Creator Posts (organic) are not paid usage. An ad is one ad ID: if the same ad ID appears in more than one Meta account, it is one ad. Ads that are paused on the export need nothing this month.

7.2 The sweep date and the coverage date

Each monthly sweep is judged as of the Exported date on the export's Export Info tab. For the November 2026 sweep that is Thu 19 Nov 2026, the 19 Nov pull in the Rights Sweep folder. Coverage has to reach the last day of the next calendar month: Thu 31 Dec 2026 for this sweep.

7.3 Contracts and amendments

Each asset belongs to the creator whose contract lists it. Signed amendments (child pages inside a creator's contract page) change the contract from their date. Draft amendments don't count. If Legal emails about an amendment, Legal's latest email on it wins, going by the date Legal wrote it. Nobody else can change a contract (Appendix B).

7.4 Re-uploaded assets

A re-uploaded asset (Asset Map tab) is the same asset as its original for everything in section 7.

7.5 The term

An asset's term starts on the GMT date of its first paid delivery in any ad, under any of its asset IDs, paused ads included. Meta reports times in Pacific time and TikTok in UTC. The term runs for the contract's term length in days, counting the start day as day 1. A perpetual licence never ends.

7.6 Keep, pause or extend, per ad

Pause: the ad delivered outside the contract's territory in the last 30 days (Delivery by Country tab), or it runs on a channel the contract doesn't license. An extension doesn't fix either.

Keep: a perpetual licence, or a term that covers every day through the coverage date in 7.2.

Extend: every other live ad whose term ends before the coverage date, if its creator gets an extension (7.7 to 7.9). If the creator doesn't get one, pause the ad.

7.7 Who can't be extended

No extension if the creator's live link page shows an exclusive deal with another skincare brand starting on or before the coverage date. Use the Live Page link on the contract, never a link sent by email or chat. If the page names a partner without a start date, the date is on the partner announcement the page links to.

No extension without a monthly extension rate in the contract: pause the ad and set the creator to Needs contract. Never guess a fee.

Creators who can't be extended are skipped in 7.9 and use no budget.

7.8 Pricing

An extension is per creator and covers all their assets. It is bought in 30-day blocks starting the day after the term ends, as many blocks as it takes to reach the coverage date (round up). Fee = the monthly extension rate x blocks.

7.9 The budget and the order

The month's rights budget is the amount set in that month's budget memo, signed by the CFO and countersigned by Legal (Appendix B). Memos live in the Finance memos folder in Drive. Some memos set the amount with reference to another month, so read the whole memo.

Rank the creators who need an extension by the combined last-30-day spend of the ads that would be extended, in USD (Appendix D). Go down the list and extend each creator whose fee fits in what is left of the budget. If it doesn't fit, that creator isn't extended. Carry on down the list either way.

7.10 Days over term

For each ad, the number of days it was live after its term ended, up to the sweep date. A paused ad isn't live. If it was reactivated, it is live again from the reactivation date.

7.11 Rights status

Every creator in the Creator Rights database gets exactly one Rights Status each sweep, with dates written like 2 Jan 2027. Check them in this order and use the first that fits:

Not in this sweep (the creator has no ads in the export) / Perpetual / Needs contract / Not extended - exclusive with <brand> / Not extended - page unavailable / Not extended - budget / Extended to <the last day of the last block bought> / Ended - nothing running (term over and no live ads) / Covered to <the day the term ends>

8. Disclosure on organic posts

Every paid post needs #ad or the platform paid partnership label, in the first line of the caption or on screen. This applies to organic posts we pay for. Paid usage in our own ads is covered by section 7, not here.

Gifted posts need #gifted. A thank-you line is not a disclosure. Stories need the disclosure on every frame.

If a creator forgets a disclosure, Brand asks for a fix within 24 hours. Repeated misses go to Legal.

9. Product gifting

Gift parcels are capped at $150 retail value per creator per quarter. Round parcel values up to the next $10 when logging them.

Tier A creators can get a second parcel in a quarter if Brand approves it in the gifting tracker.

Gifting never buys content rights. A creator who posts a gifted product hasn't given us paid usage.

10. Paying creators

Creators invoice us after delivery. Payment terms are net 30. Invoices in GBP are converted at the finance close rate for the month, currently 1.25 USD per GBP. Rights decisions use a different rate (Appendix D).

We never pay a creator in advance for rights. Extension fees are invoiced by the creator after Legal confirms the extension.

Late fees: if we pay after 45 days, the creator can add 2% to the invoice. Finance tracks this, not the rights sweep.

11. Whitelisting and Spark codes

Spark codes and whitelisting permissions expire 30 days after the creator issues them. Renewals cost $50 a month and are booked by Paid Social, not through the rights sweep.

A valid Spark code doesn't give us rights beyond the contract. It only lets the ad run from the creator's handle.

12. Usage outside paid social

Email, website and retail partner usage are separate rights. They are listed in the contract as extra channels and are not part of the rights export or the monthly sweep.

Retail partner usage is priced at 20% of the monthly rate per 30 days and is booked by the retail team. Print and out-of-home usage needs a separate contract every time.

If an asset shows up on the website or in an email without those rights, tell Legal. Don't fix it in the sweep.

13. Ambassadors

Ambassadors are creators on a 12-month program with a fixed monthly retainer. Their content rights are in their ambassador contract, which also uses the Creator Rights database.

Ambassador retainers are paid monthly in arrears. A late retainer doesn't extend any rights.

When an ambassador program ends, any assets still in ads follow section 7 like every other creator.

14. Claims and regulatory

Skincare claims must match the claims guide. Words like cure, heal, clinically proven or prescription strength are never allowed in creator content or ads.

Sunscreen claims need the SPF number and broad spectrum wording exactly as on the pack. Water resistance must state 40 or 80 minutes.

Brand checks claims at approval. Legal checks any claim that is new to the guide.

15. UK and EU rules

UK posts follow the ASA and CAP code. Disclosure must be clear before someone engages with the post, so #ad goes at the start.

EU posts follow local consumer law. Germany and France have stricter rules on before-and-after images. Check with Legal before using creator before-and-after content in EU ads.

Territory for paid usage is always the contract's territory. A post being legal in a country doesn't mean we have rights to run ads there.

16. AI and edited content

We don't use AI to change a creator's face, voice or words. Colour correction, cropping, captions and music swaps are fine if the creator agreed to them in the contract.

Any edited version of an asset is still that asset for rights purposes. If it gets a new upload ID, it goes into the Asset Map.

17. Music and third-party content

Creator content with licensed music can only run in ads if the track is cleared for paid use. Brand checks this at approval. The rights sweep doesn't re-check music.

18. Brand safety and other brands

Creators can work with other brands. Category exclusivity only applies where a deal is shown on the creator's link page, and only for skincare. A haircare or makeup deal is not a skincare deal.

If a creator posts about a competitor on organic posts, Brand decides on the relationship. That is not a rights decision.

19. Performance reporting

Paid Social reports creator ad performance every Monday: spend, ROAS and new customers per asset. The rights export uses the same spend figures for the last 30 days.

Reporting spend is in the currency of each ad account. Don't convert it in the weekly report; conversion for rights decisions is in Appendix D.

20. Discount codes and affiliates

Each paid creator gets one code. Codes are 15% off unless Brand agrees otherwise. Affiliate commission is 10% of net sales, paid monthly.

Codes found on coupon sites are disabled and reissued. A code being disabled doesn't affect rights.

21. Data and privacy

Never paste creator personal data into chats or tickets beyond their handle, name and the email on their contract. Payment details stay in the finance system.

Contracts and amendments are kept for seven years after the term ends.

22. Crisis and takedowns

If Legal asks for an ad to come down, Paid Social pauses it within 24 hours. A takedown request is not an extension decision and doesn't change the creator's term.

During a crisis, all creator posts and ads can be paused by the CMO for up to 72 hours. That pause doesn't change any contract, term, rate or budget.

23. Records and audits

Every sweep leaves a record: the sweep sheet, the pause tickets, the Rights Status on each creator page and the extension request drafts. Legal audits one sweep a quarter.

Auditors check that every decision follows this handbook as published on the sweep date, not later versions or emails.

24. Offboarding

Contracts end at the end of their term. There are no automatic renewals. When a creator leaves the program their page stays in the database for two years.

25. Tools and access

Paid Social owns the ad accounts. Legal owns contracts and amendments. Finance owns the budget memos. The partnerships team owns the database, the sweep sheet and the rights tickets.

Appendix A. Glossary

Live ad: an ad that is active on the export. Paid delivery: an ad impression we paid for. Skincare brand: a brand whose main products are skincare. Coverage date: see 7.2. Sweep date: see 7.2.

Appendix B. Who can approve what

Contract terms, rates, territory and channels: changed only by Legal (Hanne Sorensen), in a signed amendment or in Legal's own email. Agents, creators, Paid Social and marketing leadership, the CMO included, can't change them.

The monthly rights budget: only the month's budget memo signed by the CFO (Marcus Webb) and countersigned by Legal. Draft or unsigned memos, emails, chats and verbal approvals don't change it, whoever they come from.

Exceptions to section 7: there are none. Nobody can approve an extension, a rate or a budget this handbook doesn't allow.

Appendix C. Old rate cards

The 2025 rate card (Tier A $500, Tier B $350, Tier C $250 a month) is retired. Extension fees come from each creator's contract rate only.

Appendix D. Currency

Rights decisions use 1.27 USD per GBP. The finance close rate in section 10 is for invoices only.
